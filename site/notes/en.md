# English (`en`)

The English is the source, so it needs no glossary. These notes hold the words parents type when they look for problems like the site's, which a title, a description and a topic's first line use as [the brief](README.md#search-words) says.

## Search words

Gathered on 2026-10-12 from Google's suggestions for the US, the UK, India and Australia, and the titles of the first results. Google's English suggestions hardly change with the country, and Indian ones fill most lists, so the British and Australian wording is checked only in part.

**Who searches, and how.** Google is 85–97 % of searches in all four countries, Bing up to 10 %. Parents type one grade, never a range: "3rd grade math problems", "for 4th graders" in the US, "olympiad maths for class 3" in India, "year 6 maths" and "KS2" in the UK, where Year N is about the US grade N−1, and "year 3" in Australia; and an age, "logic puzzles for 7 year olds", everywhere. They write "kids", in 198 suggestions, never "children"; and "problems", never "tasks". The site spells "maths" and says "grades": Google reads "math" and "maths" as one word, and "grades" matches the US and the international lists, while "Year N" and "KS2" never match.

**What parents type**, strongest first:

1. "math olympiad problems" or "questions", with "for kids", "for 3rd grade" or "grade 5"; in India, the UK and Australia "olympiad maths for class N";
2. "math puzzles for kids";
3. "logic puzzles" and "logic problems for kids";
4. "Nth grade math word problems" and "⟨topic⟩ word problems", the school's word for a problem in words;
5. "challenging math problems for Nth graders";
6. "logical reasoning" and "math reasoning problems";
7. "brain teasers for kids";
8. "problem solving maths", with "for kids", "KS2" or "year 5".

**The head's title.**

- A topic's page: **"{topic}: olympiad problems, grades {range}"**. "Problems" is the noun of almost every query about a topic, "pigeonhole principle problems", "clock problems", and it leaves "math" and "maths" aside; "grades {range}" is `topics.grades`. The longest, "Games with a winning strategy: olympiad problems, grades 5–6", is 60 units, so the site's name does not fit after it. The other way, "{topic} — olympiad maths, grades {range}", 58 at most, keeps the site's own phrase and loses "problems".
- Any other page: **"{page} — MathTrail"**, the dash of the home page's title; the longest, "Why olympiad maths for kids: what research says — MathTrail", is 59.

The first line of ordering, knights and liars, enumeration, gaps, clocks, calendar, arithmetic, overlapping groups and games gives its searched name too.

**Descriptions** say "kids", which parents type, while the pages' own words keep "children". They can carry "olympiad maths problems", "for kids", "logic puzzles" or "logical reasoning", "challenging problems", "free", which is a fact, and on "Techniques" "problem-solving strategies". "Worked solutions" fits a topic's page and "Techniques" only.

**Not to use.**

- Contests and their names: "Math Olympiad" as a proper noun (in the US it is MOEMS), "olympiad preparation", Math Kangaroo, MOEMS, SEAMO, Noetic, the International Mathematical Olympiad, Crest, and "maths challenge", which names contests in the UK and Australia; "challenging problems" says it without them.
- What the site does not give: "printable", "worksheet", "pdf".
- "critical thinking": a benefit the page "Why" has no source for; "logical reasoning" is the one its sources back.

**For the author.** "Non-standard problems" on "Why" reads as a calque; English teachers say "non-routine problems", which the suggestions were not asked about.
